## 1. Naming & Terminology History

You specifically flagged wanting the "old naming scheme" stuff included, and there isn't one single document called that — instead there's a real *pattern* of names getting proposed, tried, and reworked across the project's history. Worth having all in one place, both so nothing old gets accidentally reused for something new, and because a couple of these are still technically open.

### 1.1 The Sundering — the naming brainstorm that came *before* the current locked names 🔵/🔒

Sync Update #3 already told you the *ending* of this story: the event is now three names across three eras (ancients' *Ruin of Echoes*, modern *Great Resonance Divergence*, future *Unison Break*), locked in Sync Update #1 and carried into the current docs. What wasn't included anywhere yet is the actual brainstorm that happened *before* that, when "the Sundering" itself was being reconsidered as too generic a name. A dedicated naming pass rejected an entire list of "obvious" fantasy-disaster names outright — The Shattering, The Cataclysm, The Calamity, The Great War, The Fracture, The Rupture, The Collapse — on the grounds that they all sound like they could just as easily be the name of a different Minecraft mod, not something with real historical weight. The standard set for a replacement name: it should feel like it was coined **after people realized they couldn't adequately describe what actually happened.**

Candidates that were seriously floated (none of these are what got locked, but they're worth having on record so they don't get reinvented later or accidentally reused for something else):
- **The Severance** — the strongest contender at the time. The pitch: not "the world was destroyed," but that something specific was *severed* — the Domains from each other, civilizations from each other, the Affinity network, even Space itself from ordinary travel. Sample lines floated: *"Before the Severance..."*, *"Three thousand years after the Severance..."*, *"Palkia sealed Space in the aftermath of the Severance."*
- **The Sundering of Reality** — keeping "Sundering" but escalating it from "fantasy war" register to "cosmological event" register.
- **The Great Severance** — a more formal/historian's-capitalization version of Severance, casually shortenable back to just "the Severance."
- **The Ruin of Ages** — implies an entire age of civilization ended, explicitly pitched as working well with the multi-eon timeline ("the civilization that existed before the Ruin of Ages...").
- **The Great Silence** — a different angle entirely: not that the event itself was silent, but that *everything stopped after it* — Domain communication, ancient records, long-distance travel, the old Affinity infrastructure, even contact with the gods. Floated as possibly being a *later historical name* rather than the event's original name.
- **The Worldfall** — floated but least developed; the pitch was "not the world falling apart, the world itself fell," direction not fully fleshed out before the thread moved on.

None of these made the final cut — the three-name resolution (Ruin of Echoes / Great Resonance Divergence / Unison Break) in Sync Update #1 is still what's locked. This section exists purely so the discarded candidates are on record rather than lost, given how much real thought went into them.

### 1.2 The deeper reveal structure behind the Sundering — not yet in any current doc ❓/🟡

A separate, more developed piece of narrative-delivery design exists for *how* the Sundering gets explained to the player, layered rather than dumped all at once. It ties into a genuinely new worldbuilding idea: **Palkia's spatial restriction doesn't just apply to the player — it applies to everyone in the world.** No one, in-lore, can freely travel between Domains anymore. That single constraint was reasoned out to have real knock-on implications worth remembering:
- No easy inter-Domain trade.
- Some civilizations might not even believe other Domains still exist.
- Ancient maps could show places with no currently-reachable routes.
- Certain resources become genuinely rare because their source Domain is cut off.
- Some Pokémon are regarded as myths simply because their home Domain is inaccessible.
- Some ancient technologies can't be reproduced because the source materials are trapped elsewhere.
- Domain progression becomes a *world-level* fact, not just something that gates the player's own quest log.
- It gives a clean, non-arbitrary explanation for why different regions ended up mastering wildly different specialties in isolation from each other — Restoration, Synthesis, Battle, Shadow/Fractured research, Agriculture, Domain studies — because knowledge literally couldn't move between them.

The proposed in-fiction delivery is intentionally minimal at the start. The player's very first document would be something like:

> **EXPEDITION RECORD**
> The world has been recovering since the event known as the Sundering.
> Long-distance Domain travel remains prohibited.
> Your expedition has been authorized to conduct ecological research within the accessible world.
> Primary directive: Observe.
> Secondary directive: Do not disturb the recovery.

...and then nothing else. No lore dump. The rest unfolds in eight discrete narrative layers, each answering one question at a time as the player progresses: (1) *what happened* — something called the Sundering occurred; (2) *what did it do* — damaged the relationships between Domains; (3) *who stopped the damage* — Palkia sealed spatial passage; (4) *why can't we travel* — the world is still healing; (5) *why are we here* — the player's people arrived during the recovery period; (6) *was our arrival harmless* — implied "probably not"; (7) *what is Resonance* — restoration isn't just environmental, it's the world trying to reestablish relationships the Sundering broke; (8) *why does Palkia finally allow passage* — because the player has demonstrated reconnection is no longer inherently destructive.

Explicitly flagged at the time as: keep "Sundering" **provisional**, don't lock the name yet, and eventually run a dedicated Cosmology/History Naming Pass — which is presumably what later happened and produced the three-name resolution in Sync #1. This 8-layer reveal structure itself, though, doesn't appear to have made it into any current doc and is worth deciding whether to formally adopt.

### 1.3 Component naming history — the chain you specifically remembered a conflict about 🔒

You asked at one point whether naming a ball component "Resonance Core" would collide with anything, and correctly remembered there'd been a problem before. There actually is a documented two-part collision:

1. **The ball's own type/Affinity component** was originally named "Resonance Core" — then renamed to **Attunement Core** (now locked, matches Sync #1's "universal blank Core, attuned afterward" mechanism) specifically because "Resonance Core" inside "Resonance Ball" read as muddy and repetitive.
2. **Separately**, a materials-table crafting item was originally named "Resonance Heart" — renamed to "Resonance Core" to avoid colliding with the player's own Resonance Hearts progression stat — and then *that* name turned out to already be claimed by the ball component above (before its Attunement Core rename). So it got renamed again, to **Resonance Nexus**, which is still sitting as pending-confirmation rather than fully locked.

So the two independent naming chains were: *Resonance Heart → Resonance Core → Resonance Nexus (pending your sign-off)* for the material, and *Resonance Core → Attunement Core (locked)* for the ball component. If "Resonance Core" comes up again for anything new, it's now claimed twice over historically — worth avoiding a third time.

Two smaller renames from the same naming-hygiene pass, both locked: **Type Gem → Affinity Gem** (for consistency with the "Affinity-" prefix already used by Affinity Fragment and Affinity Essence), and **Memory Echo → Memory Phenomenon** (a deliberate earlier correction — when "Echo Phenomenon" was floated as an alternate name later, it was specifically rejected because it would un-do that correction and overload the word "Echo," which is already doing separate work for Echo Token, Echo-Touched, and the unrelated Temporal Echo/Dimensional Echo Legendary-domain signs).

## 2. Legendary System, Domain Authority & the Virtue Hierarchy

This is a substantial, mostly-unwritten-up system that connects several things you already know individually (Resonance, Virtues, Resonance Hearts) into a single authority hierarchy for how Legendary Pokémon relate to the player. None of this appears to be in the current `world-architecture.md` or `affinity-architecture.md`.

### 2.1 The core hierarchy 🟡

The reasoning chain, as it was actually laid out: **Affinity = what are you connected to. Virtue = what have you demonstrated. Domain Authority = what has recognized you.** Legendary Pokémon are explicitly framed as *authorities over aspects of reality*, and those authorities are hierarchical — the same aspect can have a "local" authority and a "cosmic" authority. The worked example given: **Celebi** holds natural/local/aspectual authority over Time, while **Dialga** holds fundamental/cosmic authority over Time. Recognition by a Legendary isn't just "you found it and it's yours now" — it's framed as *"you have been acknowledged by an authority over this aspect of reality,"* which is a meaningfully different narrative beat than a normal capture.

### 2.2 Resonance Hearts, Resonance Aura, and Virtues 🔒/🟡

Sync Update #3 already told you the origin story for Resonance Hearts (7 of the player's 8 were stripped during the Space-breach arrival event). What's additionally on record: **Resonance Aura is a locked, distinct concept from Resonance itself** — the two are not interchangeable terms, though the exact mechanical boundary between them wasn't spelled out in what's recoverable. **Virtues** are the record of what a player has *demonstrated* — plural, multiple named Virtues appear to exist as a category (rather than one single "virtue" meter), functioning as the qualifying layer between raw Resonance and a Legendary's recognition. The exact list of individual Virtue names and what triggers each one wasn't fully recoverable and should be treated as a genuine gap, not just an omission.

### 2.3 Domain Authority as a player-facing status 🔵

Once a Legendary recognizes a player, that recognition is Domain Authority — implicitly framed as a status/achievement layer above ordinary progression, tied to a *specific* aspect of reality (Time, per the Celebi/Dialga example) rather than being a single global "you're strong now" flag. This reads as a real design direction but not a locked mechanic — there's no confirmed material on what Domain Authority actually *grants* the player mechanically.

### 2.4 Noble Pokémon — a full hierarchy layer, and its relationship to Domain Authority ❓

A broader spawn/significance hierarchy exists in the source material:

```
Normal Pokémon → Rare Pokémon → Pseudo/very rare species → Noble Pokémon → Legendary Pokémon
```

Nobles are explicitly separated from Legendaries: a Noble requires very high *local* Affinity plus a special triggering event, while a Legendary requires extremely high Affinity plus specific world conditions plus a dedicated quest/event. Functionally, Nobles read as the highest tier of *ordinary ecological restoration* before the game crosses into the supernatural/cosmological Legendary layer. The worked example: Fire Affinity rises extremely high in a region → a Noble Ninetales appears → it becomes associated with the restored volcanic ecosystem → defeating, appeasing, or earning its trust stabilizes Fire there → the ecosystem gains a permanent guardian. That's framed as **ecological authority** — *"this Pokémon has become important enough to represent or guard this restored ecosystem"* — a clean parallel to, but explicitly distinct from, the **cosmological authority** Legendaries grant via Domain Authority.

Important honesty flag on this one: there is no preserved source line that explicitly states "Noble Pokémon are part of Domain Authority." The two systems were clearly developing in parallel, philosophically similar territory, but a direct mechanical link between them was never actually confirmed — this may be a genuine memory of a conceptual bridge that was being *considered*, not one that was finalized. Worth deciding deliberately rather than assuming it was already decided.

### 2.5 Alpha vs. Apex vs. Noble — three overlapping-but-distinct significance layers ❓

A related, partially-resolved distinction: **Alpha** is a biological/physical phenotype (an individual specimen that is physically exceptional). **Apex** is ecological significance. **Noble** appears to sit in similar territory to Apex but was recovered as its own separate concept. The three are explicitly allowed to *stack* rather than being mutually exclusive — a Noble Ninetales (ecologically significant guardian) is different from an Alpha Ninetales (a physically exceptional specimen), and a Noble Alpha Ninetales (both at once) is explicitly supported by the architecture's general tolerance for significance layers overlapping. A candidate combined hierarchy was sketched:

```
Pokémon → Alpha → Noble → Legendary/Mythical → Domain Authority
```

...where Domain Authority is recognition *by* one of the higher tiers rather than a tier itself. The exact Apex/Noble boundary specifically was flagged as unresolved even during the recovery pass that surfaced all this — it's an open question, not an oversight. There's also an explicitly-parked, not-yet-answered question of whether all of Alpha/Apex/Noble should eventually unify under one umbrella term, tentatively called **"Ecological Apex Pokémon"** — noted as a thread to come back to, never actually resolved either way.

### 2.6 Guardian Syndicate & "Resonant Restoration" 🟡

A faction called the **Guardian Syndicate** exists in the source material with a described philosophy/behavior profile (the specifics of their stance and methods weren't fully captured in what's recoverable, but they read as an organized in-world group oriented around protecting or policing the restoration process rather than a background name-drop). The phrase **"Resonant Restoration"** also appears as a recurring term of art for the overall recovery process the player participates in — likely the connective phrase tying the ecological-restoration gameplay loop to the Resonance system by name. Both are real enough to be worth preserving and picking back up, but neither has a locked mechanical definition yet.

## 3. Paradox Pokémon

A fully-scoped encounter philosophy that doesn't appear anywhere in the current docs — worth treating as its own category, explicitly **not** a Fusion/Shadow/Ultra Beast/Legendary variant. 🟡

Paradox Pokémon are defined as creatures that don't belong to the world's normal timeline — temporal intrusions, not "another generation to unlock." Two flavors:

| | Past Paradox | Future Paradox |
|---|---|---|
| Framing | Primal / Ancient / Temporal Regression | Synthetic / Futuristic / Temporal Progression |
| Feel | Biological, wild, prehistoric, overgrown, instinctive | Artificial, technological, sterile, energy-based, mechanical |
| Pillar tie | Life / Nature | Industry / Technology |
| Environmental clues | Strange weather, plants growing too fast, fossils appearing, ancient footprints, Pokémon fleeing, unusual Nature's Aura readings | Machinery malfunctioning, unexplained power drains, Create machines behaving strangely, electromagnetic disturbances, metallic footprints |

The explicit design rule: Paradox encounters must **not** be gated behind simple generation unlocks — "Gen 9 unlocked → Paradox unlocked" was specifically considered and rejected as something that would spoil the mystery outright. Instead the eligibility chain mirrors normal spawn logic but for time: **Generation → Temporal Eligibility → Anomaly → Investigation → Encounter**, the temporal-track version of the normal **Generation → Type Affinity → Habitat → Spawn** pipeline. When a Paradox anomaly triggers, the Restoration Observatory is meant to report something atmospheric like *"Temporal Resonance detected"* rather than naming the Pokémon outright — consistent with the already-established atmosphere-before-identity principle used for Legendary encounters.

## 4. Titan Pokémon — Full Encounter & Narrative Design

Sync Update #3 flagged that Titan Ball mechanics existed (discovery-hook first encounter) but didn't have room for the full Titan design itself. This is a two-stage encounter system with real mythological grounding behind it. 🟡

**Stage structure**: Titans are not caught in a single encounter. The first meeting is explicitly designed to be unwinnable/uncatchable in the normal sense — it exists to hand the player their first Titan Ball (per Sync #3's discovery-hook mechanic) and establish the Titan as a real, rediscoverable entity rather than a one-off boss. On a failed capture, the Titan flees rather than resetting — it becomes a persistent, trackable world entity the player can hunt down again later, rather than an infinite respawn. (Exact fleeing behavior — same area, relocates, becomes warier — is still undecided, flagged as open in the Unanswered Questions doc already.) The actual second-stage encounter chamber reuses real existing infrastructure — the **Cobblemon Raid Dens Addon** — rather than requiring a bespoke boss-arena system to be built from scratch.

**Mythological rationale**: Titans are explicitly grounded in a Greek-mythology framing as the design's real justification for why they need to feel qualitatively different from a big rare spawn — ancient, foundational, pre-dating the current world order, powerful enough that ordinary capture methods (ordinary balls, ordinary battle-and-weaken loops) don't meaningfully apply to them, which is *why* they need their own dedicated Ball line and their own dedicated capture phase rather than reusing Legendary or Ultra Beast systems.

A partial roster of Titan candidates exists in the source material but wasn't fully recoverable as a locked list — treat this as still open rather than assume a roster was finalized.

## 5. Resonance Fusion & Synthesis — Deeper Architecture

Sync Update #3 covered Fractured Pokémon and the Poké Synchro Device in detail. This is a *related but separate* thread specifically about **Fusion's own physical-mechanics foundation**, which turns out to be more developed than what made it into the current docs, including a real branch decision point that connects Fusion and Fractured/Shadow as siblings rather than unrelated systems. 🟡/❓

### 5.1 The branch architecture 🟢 (strong reconstruction)

```
RESONANCE → RESONANCE SYNTHESIS
                    │
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐
     STABILITY               REJECTION
        │                       │
      FUSION            FRACTURED / SHADOW
        │
   RESONANCE FUSION (physical: form, type, moves, appearance, ability, behavior)
```

This is a genuinely useful piece of architecture: it means Fusion, Fractured, and Shadow are not three unrelated exceptional-Pokémon categories bolted on separately — they're the two possible outcomes of the same underlying process (Resonance Synthesis) depending on whether it stabilizes or gets rejected. Worth cross-referencing against the Shadow/Fractured design doc, since that doc doesn't currently frame Fusion as the "stability" sibling outcome of the same tree.

Separately and in parallel: **Pokémon Biology → IV Fusion** (genetic: IVs, inheritance, traits) is its own distinct track, with **Brecher's Cobblemon Fusion** already locked as its mechanical foundation.

### 5.2 The open fork: which mod actually powers physical Fusion ❓

For the *physical* half (form/type/moves/appearance/ability/behavior), **Starlight Fusion** was investigated as a candidate mechanical foundation — explicitly framed as "investigating whether Starlight Fusion can provide the physical fusion framework," not a commitment, specifically to keep the door open to using some of its fusion forms, rejecting others, inventing fully original ones, and gating compatibility by Affinity/Resonance rather than an arbitrary fixed recipe list. This creates a real unresolved question: Part 1 of the archive already committed fairly deeply to **Cobblemon Poke Fusion** as the technical backbone for custom species (there's a full worked example built around a species called Solara using it). The two were investigated somewhat independently and were never explicitly reconciled against each other. Open question worth an actual decision: is Starlight Fusion a **competing alternative** to Poke Fusion for the same physical-fusion role, or do they serve **different purposes** (e.g. one for the custom-species content pipeline, one for in-game player-facing fusion mechanics)?

### 5.3 Smaller pieces from the same dive 🟡

- **Anchor/Partner mechanic** — referenced as part of the Synthesis process, exact function not fully recoverable.
- **Synthesis Chamber** — the actual named physical structure/interface for performing Resonance Synthesis (distinct from, but likely related to, the Research Station's Replication Terminal from Sync #1).
- **Resonance Provenance** — a tracked-history concept, likely meaning a fused/synthesized Pokémon's lineage or origin record, not fully specified.
- **Convergent Species** — a proposed ecological justification mechanic for some Fakemon: unrelated Pokémon lineages independently evolving similar traits through parallel ecological pressure, rather than every unusual species needing a bespoke fusion/mutation origin story.
- **Fakemon priority policy** 🔒 — an explicit, ordered policy for what kind of new/custom species get made first: **Extra Eeveelutions → ecologically-meaningful Fakemon → regional/convergent adaptations → Resonance-experimental species → ancient/temporal species**, in that specific order.

## 6. Player Progression: Skill Tree, Ranks, Titles & Marks

A full specialization system tied to the actual **Cobblemon Skill Tree mod**, deliberately kept separate from Affinity as a concept: *"Affinity determines what you resonate with; Skill determines how you've learned to work with it."* This substantially deepens the "Player Paths of Resonance" idea (Trainer/Researcher/Ranger) that's referenced elsewhere but doesn't currently have this level of structure written up anywhere in the current docs. 🟢 for the branch concepts and governing philosophy; 🟡 for the exact node names and thresholds below, which read as illustrative rather than final.

### 6.1 The seven branches

| Branch | Progression sketch | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Researcher | Field Observation → Ecological Survey → Anomaly Detection → Resonance Analysis → Unknown Lifeform | Documentation, behavior, anomaly/Fakemon recognition |
| Ranger | Trailcraft → Habitat Knowledge → Field Survey → Conservation → Sanctuary Keeper | Exploration, ecology, active habitat restoration |
| Battler | (sub-branches below) | Deliberately not flat combat bonuses — mastery and tactical options instead |
| Scientist / Geneticist | Species Study → Inheritance → Genetic Analysis → Mutation Research → Synthesis Theory → Advanced Fusion | Breeding, genetics, ties directly into Fusion/IV-Fusion (§5) |
| Engineer | Mechanical Knowledge → Automation → Infrastructure → Resonance Engineering → Restoration Engineer | Create-based manufacturing; "Resonance Engineering" lets machines interact with World Affinity directly |
| Arcanist | Arcane Apprentice → Spellcraft → Natural Conduit → Aura Manipulation → Resonance Magic → World Shaper | Ars Nouveau/Nature's Aura-flavored; teaches *how* you practice magic, not the magic itself |
| Occultist | Spirit Knowledge → Ritual Practice → Forbidden Study → Dimensional Research → Occult Resonance | Deliberately dangerous, real tradeoffs (see below) |

**Battler sub-branches**: Trainer (obedience/command efficiency), Tactician (battle information and tactical tools), Breeder (eggs/breeding/inheritance), Specialist (synergy with a player's own Personal Affinity), Champion (advanced battle mechanics).

**The Occultist tradeoff** is worth calling out specifically since it's a genuinely different design shape from the other six: better spirit interactions, access to dangerous rituals, alternate resource generation, unique information, and unusual Pokémon encounters — balanced against a real cost, specifically increased Anomaly risk. It's the one branch designed around a genuine downside rather than pure specialization.

### 6.2 Ranks, Titles & Marks refinements 🟡

This extends the accomplishment-based rank system already referenced elsewhere in the archive, plus Resonance Mark candidates. Confirmed shape (not fully locked): a player can *earn* multiple ranks over time, but only has one active rank/path identity at once, and switching the active one is meant to avoid becoming a tedious cooldown/paperwork process. Specialized titles (Master Researcher, Master Fisher) are meant to represent genuine accomplishment rather than becoming generic RPG class labels. **Breeder** is raised as a possible additional Player Path candidate alongside the three already-established ones (Trainer/Researcher/Ranger) — flagged explicitly as not yet at the same confidence level as those three. Resonance Marks are proposed to carry a **three-layer value model** — Identity, Collection, and Resonance Interaction — giving marks real structure instead of being purely cosmetic tags, though the exact mechanical difference between the three layers wasn't specified.

## 7. Endgame & Social Systems

A cluster of systems around what a player does once the "core loop" is well underway — competitive/social structure, permadeath modes, and collection-focused alternate modes. None of this is in the current docs.

### 7.1 Championship Protocol 🟡

A full end-game structure built around a **Champion Hall** and a **Protocol Room**, with two distinct interaction modes — a **Ball Mode** and a **Hologram Mode** — for how a reigning Champion is represented/challenged. Designed with actual multiplayer succession in mind: when a Champion is defeated, there's a cooldown mechanic governing succession rather than an instant handoff, presumably to prevent title-churn in a live multiplayer server context.

### 7.2 Retirement Protocol 🟡/⚠️

A flow for formally "retiring" a Pokémon from active use — distinct from just leaving it in the PC. There's an explicit, acknowledged design tension between this and **Cobblemon Home** (the mod handling cross-save/cross-server Pokémon storage) that was never fully resolved in what's recoverable — worth treating as a real open compatibility question before either system gets built, not just a footnote.

### 7.3 Nuzlocke mode, Plushlocke/Gacha 🟡

A dedicated Nuzlocke ruleset exists with specific rules (not fully itemized in recoverable material, but understood as a real, intentional permadeath/challenge-run mode, not just "turn on hardcore"). A separate, lighter concept called **Plushlocke** was also floated, framed around gacha-style mechanics — likely a cosmetic/collection spin on the Nuzlocke idea rather than a punishing permadeath variant, though the exact mechanic wasn't fully specified.

### 7.4 Contests vs. Rallies, Gyms vs. Battle Tower 🟡

Two deliberate pairings, each distinguishing a narrative/world-integrated version of an activity from a repeatable/mechanical version: **Contests** (implied more performance/presentation-focused, Cobblemon's own Contest-adjacent systems) versus **Rallies** (a distinct, separately-named activity — exact distinction not fully recoverable, but the two are explicitly not the same thing). Similarly, **Gyms** (world-placed, narrative, tied to the region/danger-tier structure in §11.1) are explicitly kept distinct from a **Battle Tower** (repeatable, challenge-ladder, likely tied to the Battle-Points-style currency in §8).

## 8. Economy, Loot & Rewards

A full economy-pattern research pass exists (drawn from studying other top Cobblemon packs/servers) plus a locked reward-pool taxonomy for what kinds of rewards should be handed out in which contexts. None of this is in the current mod-research docs.

### 8.1 Economy pattern research 🔵 (research/reference, not locked design)

Recurring architecture observed across the top Cobblemon server ecosystems, worth C.E.R. deciding its own position on rather than importing wholesale:
- **Currency**: server-side economies commonly use something like CobbleDollars (a generic naming pattern, not a proposal to literally call it that) or full economy-overhaul mods.
- **Economy sinks**: Poké Balls, Pokémon themselves, NPC shops, cosmetics, building supplies, breeding, travel, and services are the common places currency gets spent.
- **Player economy**: GTS (Global Trade Station), player shops, and auctions are the standard multiplayer trading infrastructure. Diosesmon's GTS specifically was noted as sophisticated — filterable by Pokémon attributes, IVs/EVs, Tera type, and price.
- **Wonder Trade** — a second, separate distribution mechanism from GTS, used by Delta specifically.
- **Loot**: PokeLoot and CobbleLoot were both identified as real Pokémon-specific loot infrastructure mods, relevant to cross-reference against C.E.R.'s already-existing Lootr-based loot architecture.

### 8.2 Reward pool taxonomy 🟢 (strong reconstruction of a real locked-feeling list)

A genuinely thorough, context-matched reward taxonomy exists, organized by *what kind of activity should hand out what kind of reward* rather than one universal loot table:
- **Research/Restoration-tied rewards**: Type Gems/Affinity Gems, Type Memories, **Terra Shards**, and similar C.E.R. material-family items — chosen specifically because they already have locked verbs (Condense, Understand, Anchor) that give the reward real narrative meaning.
- **Exploration/discovery rewards**: location-unique crafting materials, following Cobblemon's own "Loot Identity" philosophy (already echoed elsewhere in the pack's Loot Sweep rules).
- **Low-stakes/frequent rewards**: rare-colored/variant **Apricorns** — explicitly tied to the Mental Economy Delegation governance principle (small satisfying pickups, not everything needs to be a big deal).
- **Research/paleontology-flavored rewards**: Fossils, including guaranteed-trait Special Fossils.
- **Repeatable-challenge rewards**: a Battle-Points-style currency modeled on the actual Battle Tower, kept distinct from one-time unlock rewards.
- **Universal rewards**: direct Pokémon EXP/Exp. Candy — the single most broadly-applicable reward type.
- **Milestone/achievement rewards**: a player-facing progression currency (if C.E.R. ends up with a Trainer Level or similar meter), kept separate from Pokémon-specific EXP.
- **Discovery-specific rewards**: a Research-point-style currency feeding directly into the existing Research system, kept deliberately self-contained rather than mixed into generic XP.
- **Care-flavored rewards**: Friendship/Affection boosts.
- **Contest/Rally/festival rewards**: regional/rare foods with real cooking-system effects (not purely cosmetic), seasoning/bait items for fishing specifically, and Poké Puffs/Poffins/Poké Cakes-equivalent items.
- **Collection/completion rewards**: PC Wallpapers, including proposed Affinity-themed custom ones — again tied explicitly to Mental Economy Delegation as a low-stakes, purely expressive reward category.

Also referenced in the same material but not otherwise covered anywhere: **Z-Crystals** and **Affinity Essence** (the latter specifically floated as a possible Fusion-catalyst material, tying back into §5), and deeper unwritten context around **Occupied Pokéballs** as a mod beyond its basic confirmation in the Master Mod Index.

## 9. World Building: Danger Tiers, Towns/Cities, and the Landmark Build Guide

### 9.1 Region-based danger tiers 🟡

A locked-feeling ecological-storytelling device for giving hostile-mob difficulty real narrative meaning instead of just being a flat difficulty slider, structured as a progression:

**Restored Hearthhome** (mostly safe) → **Abandoned settlement** (normal hostiles) → **Corrupted region** (harder mobs + Shadow phenomena) → **Ancient ruin** (unusual enemies + Anomaly mechanics) → **Legendary domain** (completely different rules).

Note: "Restored Hearthhome" appears here as the example name for the safest tier — this may be the closest thing to a "named settlement" in the recoverable material, though it reads as illustrative rather than a confirmed proper noun for an actual location.

### 9.2 Hostile mobs & boss philosophy 🟡

Explicit recommendation: keep vanilla hostile mobs as the baseline, add a small number of *highly curated* bosses, and deliberately skip building out a large generic boss-mod catalog. **Ender's Cataclysm** was specifically discussed and leaned against as a boss-mod source — not because its bosses are poorly made, but because a boss with no answer to "what does this mean in our world?" dilutes the pack's identity rather than reinforcing it. When a boss *is* included, the rule is that it should belong to an existing system rather than being generic — e.g. an Occultism boss tied to an escaped ritual (defeating it restores an area, unlocks research, reveals ancient-civilization lore), or a Shadow boss framed as "an ecosystem has become fundamentally wrong," requiring investigation before the fight rather than being just a fight.

### 9.3 Mod Audit Anti-Bloat Policy 🔒

An explicit, locked policy list (not just situational advice) for what to actively avoid when evaluating any candidate mod: generic quest spam; dozens of redundant Pokémon currencies; random loot-rarity inflation; large quantities of unrelated structures; machines that exist only to justify another machine; large weapon/armor progression chains unless directly justified; generic RPG classes/stat systems that would compete with the pack's own Skill Tree identity (§6); duplicate storage/map/economy/biome systems; and orphaned-dependency claims that weren't actually checked against the real installed JAR/dependency list. Preference order: native NeoForge solutions first; a Fabric-bridge mod only when the benefit clearly outweighs the compatibility cost.

### 9.4 Pokémon town/city design patterns 🔵

Two competing patterns were researched from other packs, relevant background for whatever C.E.R. eventually does with its own settlements: **CobbleTowns**-style packs build recognizable, pre-named Pokémon-universe towns (Pallet Town, Lavender Town, Pokémon Centers, Poké Marts) directly into world generation. Other packs instead go **server-native** — building their own original regions, cities, gyms, villages, and settlements rather than reusing existing Pokémon place names. This was flagged as "a major opportunity for C.E.R." specifically — i.e., leaning toward the original/server-native approach rather than reusing Kanto-style names — but no actual settlement names or a formal naming convention were locked. Separately, land-claim/player-territory systems (used by packs like Delta for join/create towns, land claims, player shops) were noted as the standard multiplayer infrastructure layer underneath whatever the narrative settlements end up being.

### 9.5 The Landmark Build Guide — the Observatory/World Tree scene, in full 🟢

This is real, concrete, actionable content that exists but was never pulled into the current docs — it directly supports the physical build of the pack's signature visual location, and two alternate versions of the guide exist depending on scope.

**The six zones**: (1) **The World Tree** — the central landmark, ancient/magical/glowing, potentially ~180–220 blocks tall with a massive root system, internal glowing Resonance veins, and a layered canopy; (2) **The Ritual Plaza / Restoration Observatory** — the circular ruin with 18 pillars and pools representing the 18 Affinities; (3) **Waterfall/River Valley**; (4) **Floating Islands**; (5) **The Town**; (6) **Ruins and Bridges** connecting everything together.

The Observatory specifically has **two legitimate candidate versions**, both preserved as options rather than one being chosen: a **ground-level circular 18-pillar** version, and a **floating broken-ring/archway** version with 18 illuminated Affinity sockets and a central Resonance Core. Construction hasn't decided between them yet.

Two separate build guides actually exist, covering different scope: an original **four-zone guide**, and a requested **alternate six-zone guide** (adding the Town and the Ruins/Bridges), deliberately kept as two separate documents rather than one replacing the other, specifically so they could be compared side by side. The six-zone guide includes real, actionable build guidance: realistic scale/proportions sized to what's actually buildable in vanilla+WorldEdit, material palettes per zone, a suggested build order (foundation → terrain → structures → detail pass), and specific tool guidance — WorldEdit for large-scale terrain shaping and copy/paste symmetry, structure blocks for repeating elements, and a note to check existing community tutorials for the World Tree specifically rather than reinventing that piece from scratch. The Ritual Plaza's geometry (concentric rings, evenly spaced pools) was specifically noted as simple enough that exact WorldEdit `//` commands or block-by-block coordinates could be written out directly, without needing a schematic file.

A generated key-art prompt also exists for this same scene (main menu/promotional art), written specifically to stay clear of Nintendo's trademarked Pokémon logo/Poké Ball iconography while still being recognizably "Cobblemon-style" — worth keeping on file if promotional art ever gets generated, since it already has the IP-safety language worked out.
